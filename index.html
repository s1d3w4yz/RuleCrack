Rule Chain Lab

A hashcat mask rule is a chain of small transformation steps — capitalize, substitute, append, swap, reject — applied to a word in order. Build a chain below from the full hashcat rule set, watch each step fire one at a time, and see what happens when you reorder, add, or remove a step. The chain exports as a real .rule line, matching the syntax documented in hashcat's rule-based attack reference.

Steps run top to bottom. Use the arrows to move a step and watch the trace change — order changes the output.

Reject steps don't transform the word — they decide whether the candidate survives at all (e.g. "reject unless length ≥ 8"). Once one rejects, the chain stops and the candidate is discarded. Memory steps (M 4 6 X Q) only matter in attack modes that keep a memory slot between rules; here they demonstrate the mechanic.
